# Lesson 5 guide: AI and assessment

**Time:** 75 minutes. **Students need:** a charged laptop, and a partner for one activity.

**Goal:** students understand the three AI levels, can judge whether AI use is acceptable, know they must be able to explain their own work, and agree to their responsibilities.

In the code, this lesson's ID is `L3`. Students see it as Lesson 5.

## Before the lesson

- **Check this matches the school's position.** The lesson presents Year 9 class expectations based on the school's AI levels. It doesn't quote the senior course's policy. Task sheets set the AI level, and breaches are handled under the school's usual assessment procedures. Check the wording in `src/Lesson3.gs` before the lesson each year.
- **Decide on the extra challenge.** It asks students to use an AI tool only if you allow it. Otherwise they fact-check the sample AI answer from the lesson instead.

## Timing

| Minutes | Section | Students do |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Why this matters | A quick class poll, which you run aloud |
| 12 | The three levels | Learn Levels 0 to 2, then match actions to the lowest level that allows them |
| 15 | Scenarios | Decide whether eight students' AI use is fine |
| 10 | Explain your own work | Explain short Python programs to a partner, line by line |
| 8 | Why we acknowledge AI | Why AI help is marked and recorded, with one example of marking |
| 10 | Check AI's answers | Find the mistakes in an AI explanation of code |
| 7 | Protect your information | Sort examples into safe and not safe |
| 8 | Quiz and agreement | Five quiz questions, then agree to their responsibilities |

The quiz gives instant feedback but isn't recorded. The Dashboard shows who ticked **I completed the quiz** and **I read and agree to my responsibilities**.

## Links to the rest of the course

- **VS Code's AI features:** students turned these off in Lesson 3. This lesson reminds them that grey suggestions count as AI-written code, so they're only allowed at Level 2.
- **Marking and recording AI help:** these are taught as ideas, with one example of comment markers. No format is set yet, so tell students the format for each Level 2 task.
- **Git:** the lesson says Git comes later in the year, as a way to show how work developed.

## Where students may get stuck

- **"If I acknowledge it, it's allowed":** this is the most common misunderstanding. Scenario 8 and the quiz both address it.
- **Level 1 versus Level 2:** at Level 1, AI may explain, but nothing it writes goes into the work. Pasting a fix from AI is Level 2.
- **The explain activity:** students haven't learnt all of the Python yet. That's deliberate. The point is noticing which lines they couldn't explain.

## Answers

**Lowest level:** writing your own code is Level 0. Asking what an error means, and asking how a loop works, are Level 1. Using an AI-written function, accepting a VS Code suggestion, and copying AI's explanation into your answer are Level 2.

**Scenarios:** only 2 (Noah) and 4 (Zara) are fine. The others are not fine: 1 uses AI at Level 0, 3 pastes AI code at Level 1, 5 can't explain the work, 6 copies a friend's program, 7 shares classmates' names, and 8 assumes a level.

**Check AI's answers:** statements 1 and 5 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong because `len` gives the number of items. Statement 3 is wrong because `/` can give a decimal. Statement 4 is wrong because an empty list means dividing by zero.

**Privacy:** a general question and your own error message are safe. A password, personal details, a classmate's work, photos of friends and the class list are not safe.

**Quiz:** your teacher sets the level; AI explains and you fix it; explain how it works; check it; your own error message.
